# Five-act template for short stories

A fixed-length shape for small, self-contained stories set in the world.
**Total: 72–101 lines** (a line ≈ one sentence) — roughly 1,500–2,500 words.

The five acts are a causal chain, not five equal blocks: acts 3, 4 and 5 are each a
*consequence*, so every act must be caused by the one before it. If you can reorder two
acts without breaking anything, the chain isn't tight yet.

---

## Act 1 — The Ordinary World · 16–21 lines

The longest act, because everything later has to be paid for here.

- Establish the world as a **pressure**, not a backdrop — something that grinds on people.
- Establish a character who doesn't fit that pressure, and what he wants instead.
- Introduce the **lure**: the concrete thing the want attaches to.
- Establish why he can't have it.

**End on:** the want denied. Somebody talks him down, or the way is closed.
**Check:** does the reader know what he wants, and what wanting it costs him socially?

## Act 2 — The Inciting Incident · 14–20 lines

- The world changes so the impossible becomes possible.
- He acts, against everyone's advice.
- **The result is not what he wanted.** The lure turns out to be something else.

**End on:** committed, and unable to go back.
**Check — the big one:** is this act *one event*, or have you told the whole story here?
This is the act that wants to swallow the other three. Keep it to the crossing and the
disappointment; everything after belongs to acts 3–5.

## Act 3 — The Immediate Consequence · 14–20 lines

Hours or days later.

- The new world, and its rules.
- Usually: someone who values him for the thing his own people ignored.
- **He makes a choice** — the one the rest of the story judges him for.

**End on:** the choice made, its cost not yet visible.
**Check:** does he *decide* something here, or is it all done to him?

## Act 4 — The Long-Term Consequence · 14–20 lines

Years later.

- The choice pays off. This is the high point — he gets what he wanted in Act 1.
- Show the good of it plainly, so the loss lands.

**End on:** the first crack.
**Check:** is the good outcome built on the very thing that will destroy him?

## Act 5 — The Final Consequence · 14–20 lines

- The bill comes due.
- **It must be caused by his own act in Act 2 or 3** — not by a stranger, not by politics
  arriving from offstage.
- Close on the meaning: was it worth it? Let him answer, even silently.

**End on:** the verdict.
**Check — the sharp one:** *could this ending happen if he had never acted?*
If yes, it's the wrong ending. It's a thing that happened to him, not a consequence.

---

## Using it

One file per story in `stories/`, headed with the five acts and their line budgets.
Write to the budget — the constraint is the point; when an act runs long it is usually
because it has absorbed the next one.
