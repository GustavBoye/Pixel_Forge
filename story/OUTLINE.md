# Outline

The story boiled down. One line per scene, so the shape is visible on one screen.
Chapter-level detail lives in the manuscript; this file is for moving pieces around.

---

## Spine

A boy who has been taught that he is powerless goes looking for the strength to hit
back — and is given a different kind instead.

Ulfir has been robbed, beaten, and left toothless by the occupiers while his own elders
looked away. Everything he wants is a way to answer that. He gets his father's crossbow,
uses it once, and fails; what actually saves him is a vow and a stranger's help. From
there the story hands him powers he didn't ask for — a hidden people, a prophecy, an offer
of sorcery — and ends by asking whether he has learned the lesson, in the one place it
costs him: Lily comes back carrying an occupier's child and unable to have another. He
either wants her, or he wants her unmarked by them.

**Dramatic question:** what will Ulfir do with power once he has it?
**The answer the book argues:** Baldur already said it — *"strength isn't always about how
many enemies you can defeat. Sometimes it's about standing tall when the world around you
crumbles."* The ending is that line put to the test.

## Scenes

Movement I — The Delivery
1. **Mountain path → the gate.** Ulfir arrives, proud to have made it. The southern gate
   is shut, and it never is. *(World + the boy's pride + first wrong note.)*
2. **Courtyard.** Ramdal. Supplies from Hrafvik alone; no other tribe came. The gift
   crossbows carry the occupier's mark. *(The wall is starving and abandoned.)*
3. **The feast, half-empty hall.** The pale woman on the stairs; the Haesling prank, then
   the truth — Huldus in refuge, and Ramdal hangs if it's known. *(The secret that can
   kill.)* — ○ Urba glimpsed here first; ○ Vidrik's huldu-kid story; ○ the battles east.
4. **Dawn on the wall.** Urba asleep in the snow; Ulfir covers him with his own fur.
   *(His one unforced act in the whole first movement — and the theme in miniature.)*
5. **The ancient stair.** The sealed passage, the occupier's faded mark, the clinking
   behind the stone. She vanishes at first light. *(Central mystery planted.)*
6. **Morning, great hall.** Ramdal demands men; Hrafvik can't pay it. Horn. "They are
   here." *(War begins; the boys are sent away from it.)*
7. **The old forest.** Nine hunched figures cross the path in the mist. *(It's already
   past the wall.)*

Movement II — The Cost
8. **Hrafvik.** A dead Grukk in the dirt, dead Norinheimers under the snow. Men at dawn,
   patrols north. *(Home is not safe either.)*
9. **The doorway.** Baldur leaves. The father's crossbow. *(Ulfir gets the means and the
   thesis in the same scene.)*
10. **[Chapter 6 — empty]** The winter. Patrols. No word from Wolfspine. *(Needs to make
    the silence about Ramdal and Baldur unbearable before Ch. 7 lightens the tone.)*

Movement III — The Forest
11. **The beating.** Two Bloodguards on a gnome. Ulfir aims, hesitates, steps out anyway,
    and is beaten, robbed, and put on a stump to be shot. He prays; he vows; the berries
    take their eyes; the gnome pulls him away. The mentor is dying: "Redcloaks."
    *(The real start of his story — he tries the strength he wanted and it fails him.)*

Movement IV — The Hidden World
12. **The Vibkriseer's house.** She can't save the old gnome. Lily. Gnomes have documented
    everything for 5000 years, stored in the deep mountain. Ulfir put in the dark.
13. **Healing.** Days of recovery. Lily. *(Unfinished — needs a closing beat.)*
14. **The gnome hold, east.** The body returned. Boomsticks, suspicion, a cell. Doors
    built human-sized for trading with Huldus. ○ The court — and the woman from Wolfspine
    naming him for the prophecy.
15. **Thaldrin, King of the Huldus.** ○ Stub.

Movement V — The Offer
16. **Back at Wolfspine.** ○ Ramdal, the men, something disappearing — or Baldur making it
    work. *(First scene away from Ulfir. See decision 1.)*
17. **"Do you want to become a sorcerer?"** ○ The drink.

The Return
18. **Lily comes back.** A Bloodguard child; no more children possible. Does Ulfir keep
    her? *(Everything above is built to make this cost something.)*

○ = not yet written.

**A read that would tie three of these together at once:** the clinking behind the sealed
wall is picks — gnomes, mining, keeping their records in the deep. The passage was the old
Huldu–gnome trade road (Ch. 14 says their doors are human-sized *for trading with Huldus*),
and the Bloodguard bricked it up. That single answer explains the mark on the stone, why
the Huldus at Wolfspine know the fortress's bones, and why the gnomes hold what Ulfir
needs. It costs nothing to adopt and it makes the first two chapters load-bearing.

## Where it's hollow

- **Chapter 6 doesn't exist.** Between Baldur walking away and Ulfir cheerfully picking
  berries there is a whole winter with nothing in it.
- **Ulfir is passive until scene 11.** He carries a bag, watches, and is told things. His
  only self-driven acts are covering Urba and stepping out for the gnome — the two best
  beats in the draft. Everything before should be pushing him toward acting.
- **Two inciting incidents, six chapters apart.** The war arrives in scene 6; Ulfir's own
  call doesn't come until scene 11. The war belongs to Ramdal and Baldur's story.
- **The war then vanishes** for the entire middle and only returns as a stub.
- **Scenes 15–17 are stubs**, and they carry the prophecy, the king, and the magic — i.e.
  the three things Act 2 runs on.

---

## Decisions that unlock the rest

1. **Whose story is it?** Ulfir alone, or Ulfir with cutaways to Ramdal and Baldur? Scene
   16 is the first break in POV and it can't be the only one. Answer this and scenes 6,
   10 and 16 all resolve.
2. **Where does Act 1 end?** Thirteen chapters carrying a prophecy, a king and an offer of
   sorcery is not a first act. The natural break is scene 11 — the escape into the dark
   forest, the point he can't go back to Hrafvik as the same boy.
3. **What is behind the wall?** See the read above.
4. **What is the prophecy, in one sentence?** Until it exists, scenes 14–15 can't be
   written and Ulfir's "chosenness" competes with his earned-it arc.
5. **What does "turn" mean?** Culprit says it twice in a panic and it's never answered.
6. **Does Ulfir take the drink?** If he gets magic, say what it costs, or the crossbow —
   and the whole "strength you wanted vs. strength you get" spine — goes slack.
