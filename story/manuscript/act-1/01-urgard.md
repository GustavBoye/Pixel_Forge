# Chapter 1: Urgård

The sky blazed with fiery red and gold, as though the heavens were on fire. Ulfir trailed behind his brother, watching the heavy bags sway with each step Baldur took. Then, Baldur stopped. "There it is... Wolfspine Fortress," he said.

Ulfir could hardly believe what he was seeing. The fortress loomed before him, its towering stone walls stretching so high they seemed to touch the sky. It felt as if he had stepped into a dream, each step pulling him deeper into it. But this was no dream. The weight of his bag, pressing down with every movement, was all too real. The ropes dug painfully into his skin, and snow landing in his eyes blurred his vision. Despite it all, a surge of joy washed over him.

For the first time in months, he felt something close to happiness. Only true Norinheimers ventured this far north, and he had made it. No more worrying about Bloodguards robbing him of his hard-earned cobber coins. A smile tugged at his lips, but he quickly forced it away.

Ahead, small rocks tumbled down the mountainside. One misstep on the narrow path, and he would fall farther than they had climbed all day. He focused on his steps, it wasn't the birds of Urgård he intended to feed, but the men waiting at the fortress.

They were closing in on the gate now and the scent of forgotten history filled his nostrils. The presence of countless generations who had trod these steps before him seemed to linger in the air.

The gate bore the scars of countless attacks, deep claw marks gouged into the timber. Faded runes marked the arch above, their cryptic shapes evoking a language long forgotten.

His cousin Thorild stepped forward and knocked on the gate. The sound echoed through the mountains, sharp and hollow, shattering the heavy silence that surrounded them. He could feel the weight of a thousand eyes, watching them as they stood exposed outside the gate.

Ulfir turned to his older brother. "Baldur, do they always make you wait like this?" His voice was low, tinged with uncertainty.

"No. It's strange. The southern gate is usually left open," Baldur said, his fingers still lingering on the sword's hilt, his voice edged with unease.

The silence stretched on, until the gate finally groaned. With a slow, reluctant creak, it began to open, revealing the darkened interior of the fortress.

As they stepped into the courtyard, the air carried a sense of purpose. Men clad in chainmail worked in quiet focus, some tending to fires, their faces illuminated by the flickering flames, while others methodically sharpened their blades. Just like Hrafvik this was a place of survival.

A strong voice filled the courtyard of the fortress "Well now, look who's made it up the mountain!"

It was General Ramdal. A tall man with a chest like a barrel and shoulders broad as a blacksmith's anvil. His beard was black, gray, and wild. His face bore the marks of harsh winters and even harsher battles. He was dressed in black bearskin with a heavy chainmail underneath.

Thorild smiled broadly. "General Ramdal" He stepped forward, and they embraced. The general's hug was as crushing as ever, squeezing with a strength that could crack ribs.

Catching his breath as they pulled apart. "My father mentioned you might be needing some extra supplies this month."

General Ramdal's smile faltered for a moment, replaced by a grateful nod. "He was right about that. We been running low on everything"

Ramdal studied Thorild's face, then his smile returned. "You've brought rations, havn't you?"

Thorild looked up and met Ramdal's eyes with a reassuring smile. "Lots!" he whispered. "And the other tribes?"

Ramdals smile quickly disappeared as he let out a bitter laugh. "Nobody else had shown up, also you."

One of the soldiers walked up to Thorild and clapped him on the shoulder. "I hope you brought something edible!" the man said.

Another chimed in "I can't take another week of boiled bark soup!"

Baldur and Thorild began unloading the supplies: sacks of grain, salted meat, a bundle of fresh arrows. As the soldiers gathered around, their spirits visibly lifted. The fortress, which had seemed so grim moments ago, now buzzed with a new energy.

"Who's the little one?" Ramdal asked, gesturing toward Ulfir with his massive, dirt-streaked hand.

"My little brother Ulfir," Baldur said, ruffling his brother's hair. "He insisted on coming with us."

Ulfir stepped forward, nervous but eager, and handed over the bag he had carried. "They also got you this."

Ramdal's grey eyes gleamed as he opened the sack and saw the two crossbows inside. But his smile faded when he noticed the Bloodguard symbol engraved on them.

A red claw with a drop of blood. He stared at it for a moment, his expression turning to concern. "How did you get these?"

Baldur answered with a slight smirk. "The Bloodguard gave us their blessing to deliver them to you."

Ramdal let out a short, incredulous laugh. "A trickster, that one," he muttered, shaking his head.

His smile was thin, but a flicker of concern flashed in his eyes as he looked at Baldur. "Be careful…" he warned.

The General clapped his hands together, calling for attention. "Men! Tonight we feast!"

As the dying sun sent its last fiery rays across the distant peaks, the flames in the great hall flared brighter.

Ulfir sat beside his brother and cousin, listening to the soldiers' tales of skirmishes with trolls and the relentless isolation of life at the empire's edge. For a moment, the harsh reality of their surroundings faded, replaced by laughter and camaraderie.

His gaze moved slowly over the empty expanse of the hall. The keep, once bustling with soldiers, now stood half-empty, a shadow of its former self. Only the most loyal, or perhaps the least fortunate, remained, a stark reminder of what had been lost.

To his surprise, he spotted a beautiful woman descending the old wooden stairs at the far end. She moved with an unexpected grace, her every step slow and deliberate, her presence strangely out of place among the rough, battle-hardened men.

Ulfir blinked, unsure if he'd seen her correctly. But before he could look again, one of the soldiers noticed his curious glance and leaned toward him, his voice dropping to a whisper. "She's a Haesling," he said intensely, as though revealing a dark secret.

"Comes out after dark, tricks men with her beauty. But if you pay attention, you'll see the horror beneath. In sunlight, her skin burns like ash." The soldier continued.

Ulfir turned quickly to Baldur and Thorild, eyes wide, searching for an explanation.

"Ha!" The soldier slapped his knee, grinning ear to ear. "You should've seen your face, lad!" The other soldiers around the hall chuckled.

Ulfir's heart skipped a beat as the tension broke, and the heavy silence that had settled over him shattered into laughter.

"They are not Haeslings. They are Huldus seeking refuge" another soldier said in a serious tone.

"Aren't we all?" another voice chimed in, followed by a laugh.

Ulfir looked around, his eyes catching the soldiers' faces, there was something subtly different about most of these men, something that didn't quite match the people of Hrafvik. Maybe it was just the way their eyes glowed too bright in the flickering firelight.

"If they are Huldus, how come they didn't catch the plague like the rest?" one soldier asked.

A soldier turned to Ulfir, his stare as sharp as a blade. "Don't talk about this in Hrafvik," he warned. "If the Bloodguard finds out, they'll hang them. And Ramdal, too."

The keep lit up in purple as a thunderous crack shook the mountains, and Ulfir could hear the heavy rain pouring down in torrents. One of the soldiers glanced around the hall, his eyes scanning the faces as if searching for someone. "Who's manning the wall?!"

Another soldier turned toward him. "Urba?!" they both burst out.

"Ahaha! Poor Urba. Always Urba," a third chimed in, his laughter tinged with a note of melancholy.
