# Chapter 5: Goodbye Brother

The next morning he found Baldur standing by the door, his posture stiff, his eyes far away.

"Brother," Ulfir said.

Baldur turned, his brow furrowing as if he had been waiting for this moment. "Ulfir," he said softly, his voice low but resolute.

Ulfir stood there for a moment, unsure of what to say. He had known this day was coming, but the reality of it hit him now, harder than he had anticipated.

"We may not see each other again," Baldur said, his gaze never leaving his brother. "You'll need to stay strong. For father. For Hrafvik. For yourself."

Ulfir opened his mouth to protest, but Baldur raised a hand, silencing him. "I don't want you to follow me, Ulfir. You're not ready for what's out there."

"But I can fight," Ulfir said, his chest tightening. "I can help." His fists clenched at his sides, though he knew that words alone wouldn't change Baldur's mind.

Baldur gave a short laugh, but it lacked humor. "You've always been stubborn, little brother. But there are battles you can't win with strength alone."

Ulfir's heart ached, and he glanced at the horizon, wondering if this was truly the last time he would see his brother standing beside him. He had never felt the weight of goodbye more than he did in this moment. But then, Baldur reached into his cloak, pulling out a small, worn crossbow.

"This," Baldur said, handing it over to Ulfir, "belonged to father. It's yours now. Keep it with you, always." His eyes softened. "If you can't be by my side in battle, this will keep you safe. I trust you with it."

Ulfir took the crossbow, its weight familiar in his hands. It had always been a symbol of their family's legacy, passed down through the generations, a reminder of their father's strength and resolve.

His fingers tightened around the weapon as his throat constricted with emotion. He felt the sting of tears, but he fought them back. "I won't let you down," he whispered, though he wasn't sure if he was speaking to Baldur, or to himself.

Baldur clapped a hand on Ulfir's shoulder, his grip firm, as if trying to impart every ounce of his strength into his younger brother. "I know you won't." He hesitated for a moment, his gaze softening. "Take care of yourself, Ulfir. Stay alive. Hrafvik needs you."

Ulfir nodded, unable to speak for fear of breaking down completely. His heart was heavy with both the weight of responsibility and the pain of saying goodbye.

Baldur turned and started walking away, his steps slow, purposeful. But before he was too far, he looked back one last time. "Remember, little brother," he called out, his voice carrying the wind, "strength isn't always about how many enemies you can defeat. Sometimes it's about standing tall when the world around you crumbles."

Ulfir watched him disappear into the distance, the silhouette of his brother slowly vanishing behind the mist. He could hear the sounds of the sea crashing against the shore, but it felt muffled, as if the world was holding its breath.
