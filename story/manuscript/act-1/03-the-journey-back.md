# Chapter 3: The Journey Back

As Ulfir walked back toward the great hall, his eyes were drawn to the battlements, where men moved with frantic energy. Some pointed into the valley, others dashed up the stairs, while a few hurried down, their expressions tense. More soldiers poured out of the fortress, some looking more anxious than others. One man lost his footing, slipping down the icy steps, but no one laughed. The tension in the air was thick. Their chainmails clinked softly as they ran. A melody of war.

As Ulfir entered the great hall, he found Baldur and Thorild already seated at the grand table, sharing breakfast with General Ramdal. The air was heavy with tension, their faces grim.

Ramdal spoke, his voice low and weighted. "Your father must send reinforcements. If the Grukks catch even a whiff of weakness, they'll hit us with everything they've got."

Thorild sighed, nervously running a hand through his black hair. "Yes, But Hrafvik can't bear the burden alone. The people are barely scraping by as it is. Most of them can't even afford the new taxes. What do you think they'll say if he sends their sons away?"

Ramdal slammed his hand in the table. "What will they say if their homes get's raided by Grukks?! – What about the other Chieftains?"

Thorild's green eyes grew dull, as if drained of hope, and the quiet grew heavier.

The doors of the hall banged open, the wind biting cold as it swirled inside, causing the torches to flare with life. A soldier rushed in, panting, eyes wide. "General! They are here!"

A deep, earth-shaking roar reverberated through the valley beyond the wall, reaching the great hall like the sound of an approaching storm. One of the soldiers, eyes wide, muttered in disbelief, "What was that?"

Without hesitation, Ramdal rose to his feet. "Get back to Hrafvik, and stay sharp," he ordered, grabbing his axe and heavy round shield.

The sharp blare of a horn echoed from the battlements, its mournful call piercing the fortress walls.

The trio left the great hall, making their way across the bustling courtyard where soldiers rushed toward the battlements of the fortress. They passed through the southern gate, which creaked loudly as it closed behind them, marking the start of their journey back.

A low, ominous rumble echoed across the valley, though the sky above remained deceptively calm. Ulfir imagined the crushing blows of a giant, battering the northern gate with earth-shattering force.

They rushed their journey back to Hrafvik. As they hurried back, Ulfir couldn't shake the thought of what might be unfolding at Wolfspine Fortress. Despite Baldur's reassurances that the walls would hold, a nagging unease lingered in his mind. What if they were wrong?

Lost in his thoughts, Ulfir barely noticed the shifting landscape around him. His mind replayed vivid images of the battle. The savage roars of the Grukks, the clash of steel, and the shouts of outnumbered warriors holding their ground atop the sparsely manned walls.

The imagined chaos consumed him, each scene sharper and more dreadful than the last. It wasn't until they crossed into the old forest that reality yanked him back.

As they stepped deeper into the old forest, the air grew heavy. An eerie silence hung around them, with not a sound of wildlife to be heard. A thick mist began to rise from the forest floor, and a strange, foul odor filled the air, leaving them unsettled and unsure.

Ulfir glanced at his older brother. "What's that smell?"

Baldur didn't respond, his gaze remained fixed ahead.

Thorild squinted into the trees. "Baldur, do you see that?"

Baldur glanced over, still walking without slowing. "See what?" His voice was distant, distracted in his thoughts.

Thorild's gaze locked onto the shadows of the trees. He stopped, his voice low but urgent. "Baldur. I hear something in the bushes."

Baldur, muttered, "You always hear something," his eyes scanning the surroundings.

They kept walking, but the mood had shifted. They were more alert now, walking in silence, each step careful and quiet.

The mist thickened, wrapping around their legs like a cold exhale. As rain began to pour, and the trees emerged as tall, dead silhouettes.

After a while Baldur glanced at Thorild, a hint of concern in his voice. "How come you hear all this stuff, but I don't?"

Thorild shot him a sharp look, still annoyed. "Could it be because you're not paying attention?"

Baldur chuckled, about to respond, but his words were cut off as they both stopped, holding their breath.

Further down the path a figure concealed by the mist had quickly crossed their path before vanishing into the mist again into the forest.

"Did you see that?!" Thorild hissed, as the trio quickly took cover behind some tress, anxious about getting spotted.

"Another!" Thorild whispered. as more hunched over figures crossed their path in an unaturally fast manner.

Ulfir's heart was racing, as more and more figures swiftly crossed the path. he was scanning the area behind them hoping that they would not be discovered.

"It's not humans," Thorild muttered, his voice barely audible and laced with terror.

Baldur's voice was low as they crouched behind the trees. "Four, five…" He paused, his eyes narrowing as more figures darted through the mist. "Six, seven, eight… nine."

The trio did their best to be invisible in cover of the mist and shadows. But their fear of one of them appearing next to them the rose like a storm.

"There's so many. Is it Grukks?" Ulfir whispered, his voice tight with fear.

"I don't know," Baldur replied, his tone equally uneasy. "We've never seen one before."

It was some time before Baldur finally broke the tense silence. "I think they're gone," he muttered, his voice low and uncertain.

They resumed their journey, their steps hesitant at first but growing steadier as they reclaimed their courage. Yet the terror lingered in their hearts, keeping them on edge with every rustle of the wind.

"How did they get past the fortress?" Thorild asked, his voice barely above a whisper.

"They must have climbed the mountain," Baldur replied, his expression grim. "With patrols being so infrequent, it's easier for things like that to slip by unnoticed."

"Climbed the mountain?" Thorild muttered, his voice low in disbelief. "Are you out of your mind?"

Ulfir imagined the worst: Wolfspine Fortress ablaze, black smoke rising from the great hall and Grukk pouring into Norinheim. But deep down, he held onto hope. Ramdal had weathered worse odds and emerged victorious.
